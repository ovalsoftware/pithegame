Pi Jokes

The best π jokes, dad-grade puns and math-class groaners we know — sorted into categories so you can find the right one for the moment, whether that's a classroom warm-up or a Pi Day icebreaker.

One-liners

  • π is unreasonable. It refuses to be rational.
  • Never argue with π — it goes on forever.
  • π is the only constant in my life.
  • I had a fight with a 90° angle. Turns out it was right.
  • I tried to memorize all the digits of pi. It went on a bit.
  • Cooking with π is easy. You just go round and round until the timer rings.
  • Mathematicians don't believe in luck. They believe in pi-ority.
  • π walked into a bar. The bartender said, "We don't serve transcendentals here." π replied, "That's irrational."
  • The roundest knight at King Arthur's table was Sir Cumference. He acquired his size from too much π.

Q & A jokes

  1. Q: What do you get when you divide a jack-o'-lantern's circumference by its diameter?
    A: Pumpkin π.
  2. Q: Why should you never start a conversation with π?
    A: It just goes on, and on, and on…
  3. Q: What do you call a snake that's exactly 3.14 metres long?
    A: A π-thon.
  4. Q: What's a math teacher's favourite dessert?
    A: π à la mode.
  5. Q: How many pastry chefs does it take to make a pi?
    A: 3.14.
  6. Q: What did π say to its therapist?
    A: "I just feel so… irrational."
  7. Q: Why did π fail his driving test?
    A: He couldn't stop going round in circles.
  8. Q: Why was the equal sign so humble?
    A: Because it knew it wasn't less than or greater than anyone else.
  9. Q: What's the official animal of Pi Day?
    A: The math-lete.
  10. Q: What did the calculator say to π?
    A: "You can count on me."
  11. Q: What's a mathematician's favourite kind of dance?
    A: Square dancing — but only after a slice of π.
  12. Q: Why don't mathematicians ever throw out old pi?
    A: It never goes off — it just goes on.

The classic walk-into-a-bar

A mathematician, a physicist and an engineer are each asked, "What is π?"
The mathematician answers: "π is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter — irrational and transcendental."
The physicist says: "π is approximately 3.14159, plus corrections of order 10⁻⁶ at standard temperature."
The engineer thinks for a moment, then says: "π? About 3. Call it 4 to be safe."

For the kids

  • Why did 7 eat 9? Because you're supposed to eat three squared meals a day.
  • How does π say goodbye? "See you around!"
  • What's round, sweet, and never ends? A pizza you got with friends. (Also π.)
  • What did the zero say to the eight? "Nice belt!" And what did π say? "Show-offs."
  • Why did the math book look sad? Because it had too many problems — and not enough π.
